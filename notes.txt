One important design decision

I wouldn't make the ESP32 directly responsible for every aspect of autonomous flight immediately.

I'd build it progressively:

Stage 1: Get the drone flying manually.

Stage 2: Add GPS and display coordinates.

Stage 3: Calculate distance and bearing to a target.

Stage 4: Make it hold a GPS position.

Stage 5: Add one waypoint.

Stage 6: Add multiple waypoints.

Stage 7: Add voice commands.

Stage 8: Add geofence + return-to-home + communication-loss failsafe.

That way, if something goes wrong, you know which subsystem caused it instead of debugging a giant autonomous system all at once.

And for real-world testing, start with very low altitude, open space, prop guards where practical, and simulated/bench testing before autonomous flight. GPS waypoint flight is a significantly higher-risk step than the voice-only version.

This addition also gives us a lot of useful mathematics for the handbook you asked for: GPS coordinate conversion, Haversine distance, bearing, heading error, waypoint logic, navigation PID, geofencing, coordinate frames, and position-control equations.

In one sentence:

The laptop is the mission planner and voice interface; the LiteWing is the onboard flight controller; GPS tells the drone where it is; the IMU tells it how it is moving; and the motors physically execute the controller's commands.

One more thing: I would not buy the GPS yet until we choose the exact LiteWing firmware architecture for autonomous navigation. The stock LiteWing voice-control project is designed around Python commands and the existing LiteWing flight-control system, while autonomous waypoint navigation requires us to decide whether we're extending that firmware or moving to something like Betaflight plus a companion navigation layer. CircuitDigest confirms LiteWing can be configured with Betaflight, including PID, motor, receiver and flight-mode configuration.

If we make that decision first, I can give you the 
exact BOM + wiring diagram + physical dimensions/placement + power connections rather than giving you parts that later turn out to be incompatible.
